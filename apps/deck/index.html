Who Bartr is for

We connect buyers and sellers.

Crowdfunding sites raise money for small businesses. BizBuySell sells them whole. Neither one prices a business every day, and neither lets a buyer start with 20 shares and end up with the keys. Bartr does.

What Bartr does

Six things, each one built and running.

1Connect buyers and sellers

Every business we find is on the site before its owner has heard of us. A buyer clicks "make an offer"; Bartr writes the owner a short letter. When the owner accepts, the business lists and 3,000 shares go on sale.

2Find small businesses

Search in plain words, "laundromat in Pittsburgh". Google Places finds them, Querit reads the web, Grok builds a profile where every number keeps the sentence it came from. 53 real Pittsburgh businesses are already priced.

3Appraise and price with a range

Five valuation methods, Grok's research and K2's second opinion, each with its own typical miss, blended into one value and a range. The owner's asks and buyback come straight from that range.

4Run the market

Asks and bids clear in rounds, one price for everyone in the round. Our value updates from what buyers paid and the owner requotes. The portfolio page shows the gap on every business and sizes your stake to your risk level.

5Keep it fair

Price checks, no trading with yourself, size limits, a 10% band, halts after two band hits, proportional fills, an audit log that is never edited. All in the engine, all tested.

6Watch every trade

Rules scan the last 20 rounds of every market for wash trades, spoofing, pumps and concentration. Grok and K2 review each flag separately. Grok also attacks the market as red team so the rules get tested. Admins see it all on one dashboard.

How we price it

and how far off we might be

Five ways to value a business. Each one comes with its own typical miss.

1. What one method says
$$\ln V_k = \mu_k \pm \sigma_k$$
The income method says Squirrel Hill is worth $589k, and that method is usually off by about 25 percent. We work in logs so 2x too high and 2x too low count as the same size miss.
2. Combining five answers
$$\mu = \frac{\sum_k w_k\,\mu_k}{\sum_k w_k}, \qquad w_k = \frac{1}{\sigma_k^2}$$
Each method gets a vote weighted by one over its miss squared. Income (miss 0.25) gets 16 votes, Grok's number (0.35) about 8, the category base rate (0.70) gets 2. The blend lands at $570,768.
3. How sure is the blend
$$\sigma^2 = \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sum_k w_k}}_{\text{each method's own miss}} + \underbrace{\frac{\sum_k w_k(\mu_k-\mu)^2}{\sum_k w_k}}_{\text{how far apart they land}}$$
Two things go wrong: every method is a bit off, and the methods disagree. We add both. Squirrel Hill's three methods land between $469k and $589k, close together, so the total miss is 0.22.
4. What we show
$$[V_{20},\ V_{80}] = e^{\mu \mp 0.84\,\sigma}, \qquad \sigma \ge 0.12$$
A range, never a bare number. 0.84 sigma is where the 20th and 80th percentiles sit on a bell curve: $570,768 times 0.83 is $475k, times 1.20 is $686k. We never claim to be surer than 12 percent.

Where each miss comes from

Today's numbers come from BizBuySell's 2026 tables. We also have a check that takes real listings, hides the asking price, runs each method and records the miss. Once it has run on enough listings, measured misses replace this table.

How the market works

One price per round. The owner is on the other side.

The owner is the seller. There is no market maker.

The owner's asks. 3,000 shares (30 percent) in five lots of 600, at five prices just above our value. For Squirrel Hill, value $57.08: lots at $58.67, $60.76, $63.03, $65.59 and $68.62. A shakier valuation spreads those prices wider.

The owner's buyback. The owner also stands ready to buy up to 1,000 shares back at $47.48, about 17 percent under our value. That bid is in the book from the first minute.

One price per round. Every 10 to 60 seconds, faster when the book is busy, all orders clear at once at a single price. Everyone in that round pays it. Between rounds our value updates from what buyers paid, and the owner's asks and buyback move with it. Bartr holds no shares and takes no side.

Which price does a round clear at?

$$p^\star = \arg\max_{p}\ \min\{\underbrace{D(p)}_{\text{shares buyers want at } p},\ \underbrace{S(p)}_{\text{shares offered at } p}\}$$

Try every price. At each one, count how many shares buyers would take and how many sellers would give. Clear where the smaller of the two is largest. In round 1 of the demo, buyers wanted 305 shares at $58.67 or above and the owner offered 600 there, so 305 traded at $58.67, and Jonas, who bid $64.20, paid $58.67 too.

Fairness

Built into the engine, covered by tests

One price. No head start.

Nothing matches between rounds, so a faster connection gets you nothing. Two rounds in a row at the band edge halt the market for one round. The owner can only change quotes between rounds, never bids above its buyback price and never asks below its lowest ask, so the account holding 30 percent of the shares cannot chase the price. Every order carries a server timestamp and lands in the audit log.

Portfolio

Iron City Precision Machine, McKees Rocks

Every suggestion shows the gap between our value and the market price. A slider decides how hard to lean on it.

The gap. Our value next to the last traded price. We only suggest businesses trading below our value; the gap is what you stand to gain when the price catches up.

How much to buy. The Kelly formula turns the gap and our uncertainty into a share of your cash. A big gap with a sure value gets more; a big gap with a shaky value gets less. No single business ever gets more than 20 percent.

Your risk level. A slider from 0.25 to 1.0 scales that share. Default is half, because our value is an estimate. Move it and the gain and the possible loss move with it.

How much of my cash should go in?

$$\text{gap} = \ln\frac{\text{our value}}{\text{price}}, \qquad \text{share of cash} = \text{slider} \times \frac{\text{gap}}{\sigma^2}, \quad \text{capped at } 20\%$$

Q&A · Market

Is there a real market behind the demo?

QWhy does this need to exist now?
Ownership is about to move. The US has 36.2 million small businesses. Project Equity estimates 2.9 million businesses have owners aged 55 or older, supporting 32.1 million jobs and $6.5 trillion in annual revenue. Better web data and models now make the hidden supply searchable before an owner hires a broker.
QIs 36.2 million really your TAM?
No. It is the scale of the information gap, not a revenue forecast. Our first reachable market is profitable employer businesses in repeatable local categories, beginning with Pittsburgh laundromats, car washes, service companies, and small manufacturers.
QWhat proves buyer demand?
Existing marketplaces already attract buyers. BizBuySell reports more than 4 million visits each month, while only 2,117 transactions were reported in Q2 2026. Its own report says demand exceeds the supply of acquisition-ready businesses.
QWhat is the narrow first wedge?
Acquisition search in one dense city. Help serious buyers find and compare businesses that are not advertised for sale. Owner-approved fractional trading comes later through a regulated partner.

Sources: SBA 2025 Small Business Profile. Project Equity, 2025. BizBuySell Q2 2026 Insight Report.

Q&A · Users and consent

Who wants this, and why would owners cooperate?

QWho is the first paying customer?
The first user is a serious buyer or acquirer. They pay for broader discovery, evidence-backed comparison, and a qualified path to contact. The owner becomes a customer only after real interest exists.
QWhy would an owner sell only 30%?
Partial liquidity lets an owner take cash out without giving up control. Thirty percent is a demo parameter, not a universal answer. The real percentage would depend on owner goals, governance rights, tax structure, and offering rules.
QCan you publish a business without permission?
We can organize public facts, but we should not imply endorsement. An unclaimed page must say that Bartr produced the estimate, provide sources, accept corrections and opt-outs, and prohibit trading until the owner verifies and accepts the listing.
QAre you impersonating the owner with the owner persona?
No production system should do that without authorization. The current persona is a demo simulation grounded in the company file. A real product would label it as a Bartr assistant until the owner claims the page and authorizes responses.

Q&A · Competition

Why does an existing platform not already solve this?

QWhy not just use Google Maps?
Maps finds storefronts, not investable businesses. It does not assemble ownership evidence, operating estimates, valuation ranges, comparable prices, an offer workflow, or diligence requirements.
QWhy not BizBuySell?
BizBuySell starts after an owner decides to sell. Bartr starts with the much larger set of businesses that are not listed, prices uncertainty, and lets qualified buyer interest begin the owner conversation.
QWhy not StartEngine or another crowdfunding portal?
Crowdfunding primarily helps an issuer raise money. Bartr begins with acquisition discovery and valuation. Reg CF securities also generally carry a one-year resale restriction, and a funding portal cannot give investment recommendations or operate a secondary exchange.
QWhat stops an incumbent from copying this?
The code alone is not a moat. Defensibility would come from verified owner relationships, a growing record of valuation errors and completed transactions, category-specific evidence, and regulated distribution partnerships.

Source for crowdfunding restrictions: US Securities and Exchange Commission, Regulation Crowdfunding guides.

Q&A · Valuation

How can you price a private business with incomplete data?

QHow can you value a business without its books?
We show an estimate and a wide range, not a certified valuation. Before owner verification, the model uses public evidence, category multiples, observable proxies, and independent model opinions. Missing evidence increases uncertainty. Trading stays closed until the owner accepts the listing.
QHave you proven that the estimates are accurate?
Not yet at production scale. We built a holdout calibration path that hides known listing prices and measures each estimator's error, but we do not yet have enough completed observations to claim empirical calibration. That is an explicit next validation milestone.
QWhat stops Grok from hallucinating revenue?
A number only enters verified evidence when the exact supporting sentence, source URL, unit, and date are present. Grok is one estimator, not the final answer. K2 gives a blind second opinion, deterministic methods remain in the blend, and disagreement widens the range.
QDoes market price create a circular valuation?
It can if thin trades receive too much weight. The model begins with an evidence-based prior and weights market updates by volume. Production should also cap the influence of concentrated or flagged volume and keep the model value separate from the traded price.
QWhere does the Kelly fraction come from?
From the log-wealth derivation on the board, not from a blog post. Wealth after a win is W(1+bf), after a loss W(1-f). Maximizing E[ln W] gives f* = (pb-q)/b. We use half of that, capped at 20 percent, because the edge is estimated. Scroll the paper on the next slide.

Q&A · Market design

What happens when a tiny private market meets reality?

QWhere does liquidity come from?
In the demo, the owner posts a finite ask ladder and a finite buyback bid. Bots make the demonstration active. Production cannot promise liquidity. It must show available size, funding, quote age, and transfer restrictions clearly.
QWhat if the owner's buyback money runs out?
The bid disappears when its funded quantity is exhausted. A production bid should be limited by prefunded or verified capital. We should call it an owner repurchase order, never a guaranteed exit.
QWhy a batch auction instead of normal continuous trading?
A batch gives every participant in the round one clearing price. Speed does not decide priority, aggressive orders receive price improvement, and the engine chooses the price that executes the most volume. That fits a thin local market better than a race for the first quote.
QCan a few users manipulate the price?
Yes, thin markets remain vulnerable. Bartr reduces the risk with identity controls, self-trade prevention, position limits, a 10% band, proportional fills, concentration rules, halts, and human review. Surveillance reduces risk. It does not eliminate it.

Q&A · Regulation

Can Bartr legally sell and trade these shares?

QAre these shares securities?
Yes. A live-money product cannot treat 10,000 shares as a software abstraction. Every offer and sale needs registration or an exemption, and matching secondary orders generally requires regulated market infrastructure.
QWhat is the realistic launch structure?
Bartr supplies discovery, valuation, auction technology, and surveillance. A registered intermediary conducts the offering. A broker-dealer-operated ATS executes eligible secondary trades. A transfer agent and custodian maintain ownership and assets, with KYC and AML controls.
QDoes Regulation Crowdfunding solve everything?
No. Reg CF can let an issuer raise up to $5 million through a registered intermediary, but the securities generally cannot be resold for one year. It does not automatically cover an owner's personal 30% cash-out. Reg A or another exemption may fit that structure better.
QCan Bartr give Kelly-sized recommendations?
Personalized recommendations about specific securities may be investment advice. A funding portal is prohibited from making recommendations. Production would need a broker-dealer or adviser structure, or would limit Bartr to user-directed, non-personalized analytics.

Sources: SEC Regulation Crowdfunding guide. SEC Alternative Trading System list and guidance. SEC investment adviser interpretation. This slide is preparation, not legal advice.

Q&A · Investor rights

What does an investor actually own?

QWhat legal rights come with one share?
The demo models economic units, not a finished security. A real offering must define voting rights, distributions, information rights, transfer restrictions, dilution, repurchase terms, and priority relative to debt and the owner's retained shares.
QHow does an investor receive returns?
That depends on the security design. Returns could come from declared distributions, a later repurchase, or a sale. Bartr should not imply automatic dividends or price appreciation before the operating agreement and disclosure documents define them.
QWho maintains the cap table and tax records?
A production transfer agent should maintain the official holder record. A tax and fund-administration partner would handle statements and any K-1 or 1099 obligations. MongoDB is the app ledger today, not the final legal ownership system.
QDoes owning 20 shares make acquisition easier?
It creates information and a relationship, not a right to buy the company. The owner still chooses whether to negotiate. The LOI is nonbinding, the final price comes from diligence and negotiation, and a licensed M&A process may be required.

Q&A · Business model

How would Bartr become a business?

QHow do you make money?
The first hypothesis is paid acquisition intelligence for serious buyers. Later revenue could include owner verification and valuation services plus regulated transaction fees shared with licensed partners. Bartr should not earn a trading spread because it does not trade against users.
QHow do you solve the marketplace cold start?
Discovery creates useful inventory before owners join, but trading stays closed. Qualified buyer interest generates a specific owner outreach. Accepted owners create the first verified supply. Density in one city and a few categories matters more than national coverage.
QHow expensive will owner acquisition be?
We do not know yet. The key experiment is the funnel from qualified buyer interest to owner response, verified financials, and accepted listing. Generic cold outreach would be expensive and damage trust.
QWhat metrics would prove the business?
Owner response and verification rates come first. Then measure valuation error, time to first qualified offer, cost per verified business, repeat buyer usage, and real liquidity without simulated traders.

Q&A · Technical truth

What is real, and what was built for the demo?

QWhat actually works end to end?
The FastAPI service runs discovery jobs, valuation, order books, batch clearing, belief updates, Kelly sizing, acquisition documents, rules, model reviews, WebSockets, and audit events. The Next.js app uses those routes when configured.
QWhat is simulated or preloaded?
The demo includes seeded companies, cached research, 20 bot traders, planted manipulation, $100,000 demo balances, and X-Demo-User identity. No real money or legal securities settle.
QCan the current architecture scale?
Not as a production exchange. It deliberately runs one API worker with an in-process scheduler and WebSocket hub. Production needs durable jobs, atomic settlement, separate market workers, reconciliation, disaster recovery, and load testing.
QWhat happens when Grok, K2, or Querit fails?
Interactive paths have deterministic fallbacks and cached outputs. Search preserves partial results. Research calls can return unavailable rather than invent evidence. The readiness endpoint exposes provider status and recent calls.

Q&A · Security and reliability

How much protection is real today?

QCan someone create many accounts and manipulate a market?
Yes in the demo because identity is intentionally lightweight. Production requires broker-level customer identification, beneficial-owner checks, sanctions screening, linked-account detection, funding controls, and device or network signals.
QWhy trust AI to police manipulation?
AI does not enforce trades. Deterministic rules create the flags. Grok and K2 independently explain and grade the evidence. Disagreement is shown to a human rather than averaged away.
QIs the audit log truly immutable?
It is append-only through the application today, not cryptographically immutable. Production needs restricted write roles, retention controls, tamper-evident hashes or WORM storage, independent backups, and reconciliation with broker and transfer-agent records.
QHow do you protect private financial data?
The current demo mostly uses public and seeded data. Production needs field-level access control, encryption, consent records, redaction, retention limits, vendor review, incident response, and strict separation between public estimates and owner-provided documents.

Q&A · Hardest questions

What could make the entire thesis wrong?

QWhat is the biggest unproven assumption?
That owners will exchange verified financial data and partial equity for qualified demand. Search and valuation are technically feasible. Owner trust and conversion determine whether a marketplace exists.
QWhat results would make you stop?
We would reconsider the exchange model if owners reject partial liquidity, verified valuations remain too wide to support decisions, buyers do not return, or regulatory cost overwhelms the economics of a typical $350,000 transaction.
QCould a public estimate harm a local business?
Yes. That is why estimates need ranges, evidence, clear Bartr attribution, correction and opt-out paths, and no trading before owner consent. Sensitive inferred financials may need to remain private until verification.
QWhat are the next concrete proofs?
Interview owners and acquisition buyers. Validate estimates on held-out real transactions. Secure a broker, ATS, and securities-counsel design. Run a clearly labeled paper market. Convert the first five owner-verified Pittsburgh businesses.
