How we price it

and how sure we are

Five ways to value a business. Each one says how far off it tends to be.

1. Each method: an estimate and an error bar$$\ln V_k = \mu_k \pm \sigma_k$$
2. Blend them, trusting the tighter ones more$$\mu = \frac{\sum_k w_k\,\mu_k}{\sum_k w_k}, \qquad w_k = \frac{1}{\sigma_k^2}$$
3. Doubt has two parts, and we add both$$\sigma^2 = \underbrace{\frac{1}{\sum_k w_k}}_{\text{the methods' own error}} + \underbrace{\frac{\sum_k w_k(\mu_k-\mu)^2}{\sum_k w_k}}_{\text{how much they disagree}}$$
4. Show a range, never a bare number$$[V_{20},\ V_{80}] = e^{\mu \mp 0.84\,\sigma}, \qquad \sigma \ge 0.12 \ \text{always}$$

Where each σ comes from

σ is how far off a method usually lands, in log terms: ±25% is σ 0.25. Today's values are priors from the BizBuySell 2026 tables. The calibration routine hides the asking price on real listings, runs each method, and records the miss; once it has run on scraped listings, measured misses replace the priors. A method that misses more gets less weight.

Listing price  it is actually for sale±15%σ 0.15
Income  owner earnings x category multiple; ±40% if earnings are inferred±25%σ 0.25
Model opinion  Grok's number when we have one; wider if K2 disagrees±35%+σ 0.35+
Proxy  revenue guessed from staff, machines, reviews±60%σ 0.60
Base rate  category median in that state±70%σ 0.70

Squirrel Hill: income $589k, model opinion $560k, base rate $469k agree closely, so σ lands at 0.22 and the range is $475k to $686k. Bloomfield Coin Laundry has only a Google pin: two weak methods, σ 0.82, range $96k to $384k. The page shows both.

Logs because value misses are multiplicative: 2x high and 2x low count the same. 0.84σ is where the 20th and 80th percentiles of a bell curve sit. Multiples and medians from BizBuySell 2026, sold price taken as 0.88 x asking. A method runs only when its evidence exists.

How the market works

Frequent batch auction with owner liquidity

The owner is the seller. There is no market maker.

Ask ladder. 3,000 shares (30%) offered at five prices a little above the model value, the P55 to P80 points of its range. A shakier valuation means a wider owner spread.

Buyback floor. The owner stands to buy back up to 1,000 shares at P20, about 17% under the model value.

One price per round. Every 10 to 60 seconds, faster when the book is busy, it clears at the price where the most shares change hands. Everyone in that round pays the same price. Between rounds the owner requotes from the updated model. Bartr holds no shares and takes no side.

$$p^\star = \arg\max_{p}\ \min\{\underbrace{D(p)}_{\text{shares buyers want at } p},\ \underbrace{S(p)}_{\text{shares offered at } p}\}$$

Anti arbitrage

Enforced in the engine, covered by tests

One price. No head start.

No continuous matching, so speed buys nothing. Two band hits in a row halt the market for a round. The owner requotes only between rounds, never bids above its floor, never asks below P55, so the account that holds the float cannot chase the price. Server timestamps only; every order event lands in the append only audit log. Tested in apps/api/tests.

Portfolio

Find the edge. Size it. Keep an exit.

Edge on every suggestion. Our model value next to the market price, on every row. Only names priced below our value are suggested.

Your risk, your dial. The Kelly multiplier slider (0.25 to 1.0) sizes every stake to your appetite. Half Kelly by default, because the edge is an estimate.

An exit that stands. The owner's buyback bid is in every book: up to 1,000 shares at P20.

$$\underbrace{\mu = \ln\tfrac{v}{p}}_{\text{gain if price meets value}}, \qquad \underbrace{f = m \cdot \tfrac{\mu}{\sigma^2}}_{\text{share of bankroll, capped at 20\% a name, 80\% overall}}$$

Bet more when the gap is big and the value is sure. Bet nothing when price equals value. Never more than a fifth on one name. \(m\) is the slider.

Security

Last 20 rounds of every market, plus a Grok red team that attacks it

Rules scan every trade. Two models review every flag.

●  Flagged  wash trading, high

Rules: wash_a and wash_b have traded 9 times and only with each other. Volume prints, ownership never changes.

Grok  explains

"Alternating sides at the clearing price with no other counterparties. No economic purpose; treat as manipulation."

K2  agrees, high

If the two models disagree the flag is marked disputed and both opinions show. Every event is in the append only audit log.

Red team  Grok attacks

Grok plays the manipulator: it plans a wash, spoof or pump and runs it through bot accounts. The rules and the reviewers must catch it. Every attempt is logged.

Grok inside Bartr

grok-4.6 through the xAI API, twelve call sites, one llm.py

Grok reads, appraises, reviews, attacks, and explains.

1. Reads and appraises

Querit fetches pages; Grok returns a structured profile where every number carries a source URL and its quote. The appraiser then researches the business with Grok's web search and returns a value, a confidence and sources; K2 gives an independent number.

2. Kept only if provable

Bartr keeps a fact only if the quote appears verbatim in the fetched page. Financial numbers must be in USD, dated, and appear in the quote. A fact Grok marks inferred can never become a financial input. This is what stops a hallucinated revenue from becoming a price.

3. An opinion with doubt built in

The appraisal is one of five estimators. Its weight comes from its stated confidence: σ = 0.35 + 0.35 (1 − c). If K2 disagrees, σ widens by half the gap. A confident wrong number cannot dominate the price.

4. Reviews flags and attacks the market

Each rule flag goes to Grok and to K2 in separate calls; neither sees the other. As red team, Grok plans a wash, spoof or pump and runs it through bot accounts so the rules are tested against a real adversary.

5. Talks to the user

Chat agent with tools (search, company, book, place order, suggest); owner persona you can question on a company page; market narrator; portfolio profile parser and the "why" on each suggestion; LOI and diligence checklist drafting; a market health memo. iMessage talks to the same agent.

Never a single point of failure

Every call goes through one function with a provider switch, so K2 takes any role by changing one argument. Every call has a deterministic fallback, so the demo never hangs on a key. /readiness lists each feature and its recent calls.

Grok runs inside the product through the API; it did not write the code. scripts/grok_smoke.py exercises every call site. Seeded demo companies carry a hand entered opinion until the appraiser is run on them.
